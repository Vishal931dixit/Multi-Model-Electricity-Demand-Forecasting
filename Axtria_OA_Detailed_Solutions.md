### Q1: Cubical Die Probability
- **Source Pages**: Page 56
- **Question Statement**:
  > A cubical die is marked 'x' on three of its faces, 'y' on two of its faces, and 'z' on the remaining face. If the die is rolled twice, what is the probability that the outcome of the first roll is the same as that of the second roll?
- **Given Options**:
  - A) $7/18$
  - B) $1/6$
  - C) $13/36$
  - D) $11/18$
- **Detailed Solution**:
  1. A standard cube has 6 faces.
  2. The probabilities of rolling each face on any single toss are:
     $$P(x) = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$$
     $$P(y) = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$$
     $$P(z) = \frac{1}{6}$$
  3. The two independent rolls have the same outcome if both result in $x$, both in $y$, or both in $z$:
     $$P(\text{same}) = P(x, x) + P(y, y) + P(z, z)$$
     $$P(\text{same}) = [P(x)]^2 + [P(y)]^2 + [P(z)]^2$$
     $$P(\text{same}) = \left(\frac{3}{6}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{2}{6}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^2 = \frac{9 + 4 + 1}{36} = \frac{14}{36} = \frac{7}{18}$$
- **Correct Answer**: **$\mathbf{7/18}$ (Option A)**

     $$C_Y = \frac{15600 \times 3}{4} = 3900 \times 3 = \text{Rs. } 11,700$$

### Q15 / Q46: Sumith, Punith & Vinith Partnership

- **Correct Answer**: **3 (Option A)**

### Q46: Haritha Round-Trip Average Speed
- **Source Pages**: Page 33
- **Question Statement**:
  > Haritha travelled from Pune to Kolkata at a speed of $50\text{ km/hr}$ and returned from Kolkata to Pune at a speed of $75\text{ km/hr}$. Find the average speed of Haritha for the entire journey.
- **Given Options**:
  - A) $58\text{ km/hr}$
  - B) $50\text{ km/hr}$
  - C) $60\text{ km/hr}$
  - D) $55\text{ km/hr}$
- **Detailed Solution**:
  1. For an equal distance journey with forward speed $v_1$ and return speed $v_2$, average speed is given by the harmonic mean:
     $$v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{2 v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}$$
  2. Substituting $v_1 = 50\text{ km/hr}$ and $v_2 = 75\text{ km/hr}$:
     $$v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{2 \times 50 \times 75}{50 + 75} = \frac{7500}{125} = 60\text{ km/hr}$$
- **Correct Answer**: **$\mathbf{60\text{ km/hr}}$ (Option C)**

### Q48: Two Guns Fired & Approaching Train Speed

- **Question Statement**:
  > Two guns are fired from the same place at an interval of 26 minutes, but a person sitting in a train approaching the place hears the second shot 24 minutes after he heard the first shot. Find the speed of the train, given that sound travels at $330\text{ m/s}$.

  - B) $25\text{ m/s}$
  - C) $22.8\text{ m/s}$

- **Detailed Solution**:
  1. The second gun was fired 26 minutes after the first.
  2. The person hears it after 24 minutes because the train has travelled closer to the firing point.

     $$D_{\text{train}} = D_{\text{sound}}$$
     $$v_{\text{train}} \times 24 = v_{\text{sound}} \times 2$$
  4. Solving for $v_{\text{train}}$:
     $$v_{\text{train}} = v_{\text{sound}} \times \frac{2}{24} = \frac{v_{\text{sound}}}{12}$$
     $$v_{\text{train}} = \frac{330\text{ m/s}}{12} = 27.5\text{ m/s}$$
- **Correct Answer**: **$\mathbf{27.5\text{ m/s}}$ (Option A)**

### Q50: Rectangle Wire Deformation Area Change
- **Source Pages**: Page 50
- **Question Statement**:
  > A rectangle is made by tying the ends of four broken copper wires to each other. One vertex is pulled outwards along a straight line (three vertices remain in the same place), so that length increases by 20%. By what percentage does the area of the quadrilateral increase?
- **Given Options**:
  - A) 44%
  - B) 10%
  - C) 20%
  - D) Cannot be determined
- **Detailed Solution**:
  1. Let the original rectangle have vertices $A(0,0)$, $B(L,0)$, $C(L,W)$, $D(0,W)$.
  2. Original Area $= L \times W$.
  3. Three vertices ($A, B, C$) remain fixed. Only the fourth vertex $D$ moves along the line extending $CD$ such that side $CD$ increases from $L$ to $1.20L$ ($\Delta L = 0.20L$).
  4. The original rectangle can be split into two triangles by diagonal $AC$:
     - Triangle $ABC$: Area $= \frac{1}{2} LW$ (unchanged).
     - Triangle $ACD$: Area $= \frac{1}{2} L W$.
  5. When $CD$ extends by $20\%$ to $1.20 L$ while height $W$ remains unchanged:
     $$\text{Area}(\Delta ACD') = \frac{1}{2} (1.20 L) W = 0.60 LW$$
  6. New Total Area $= 0.50 LW + 0.60 LW = 1.10 LW$.
  7. Percentage increase in area:
     $$\frac{1.10 LW - 1.00 LW}{1.00 LW} \times 100\% = 10\%$$
- **Correct Answer**: **10% (Option B)**

### LR Puzzle Set: Eight Students Row & Height Ordering
- **Source Pages**: Pages 9, 10, 11, 13, 55

#### Given Rules & Constraints:
1. Eight students: $A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H$ standing in a row facing South.
2. Distribution of classes: Three students from Class 8th, three from Class 9th, and two from Class 10th.
3. Height clues (Rank 1 = tallest, Rank 8 = shortest):
   - $A$ is the 2nd tallest ($A = \text{Rank 2}$) but **not** from Class 8th.
   - $E$ and $B$ are from Class 9th.
   - $C$ is shorter than $G$ but taller than $F$ ($G > C > F$), and $C$ is **not** from Class 10th.
   - $B$ is the 4th tallest ($B = \text{Rank 4}$), and $H$ is shorter than $B$ ($B > H$).
   - Neither the shortest (Rank 8) nor the 2nd shortest (Rank 7) are from Class 8th.
   - $H$ is taller than $G$ ($H > G$), and $D$ is shorter than $A$ ($A > D$).

#### Step-by-Step Deduction:
1. **Height Ordering**:
   - From $H > G$ and $G > C > F$, we get the strict chain: $H > G > C > F$ (4 people).
   - Since $B > H$ and $B = \text{Rank 4}$, positions 5, 6, 7, 8 must be these 4 people:
     - **Rank 4**: $B$
     - **Rank 5**: $H$
     - **Rank 6**: $G$
     - **Rank 7**: $C$
     - **Rank 8**: $F$
   - Now consider positions 1, 2, 3:
     - Rank 2 is $A$.
     - Remaining students are $D$ and $E$.
     - Clue states $D$ is shorter than $A$ ($A > D$), so $D = \text{Rank 3}$.
     - Therefore, **Rank 1 (tallest overall)** must be **$E$**.
   - Complete height ranking (Tallest to Shortest):
     $$\mathbf{E (1) > A (2) > D (3) > B (4) > H (5) > G (6) > C (7) > F (8)}$$

2. **Class Allocation**:
   - Class 9th already has $E$ and $B$.
   - Neither Rank 8 ($F$) nor Rank 7 ($C$) is from 8th. $A$ is not from 8th.
   - $E$ and $B$ are 9th, so the only remaining candidates for Class 8th are $D, H, G$.
   - Since there are 3 students in Class 8th, they must be:
     $$\text{Class 8th} = \{D, H, G\}$$
   - Since $C$ cannot be from 10th and is not from 8th, $C$ must be the 3rd student in 9th:
     $$\text{Class 9th} = \{E, B, C\}$$
   - That leaves the 2 students of Class 10th:
     $$\text{Class 10th} = \{A, F\}$$

  - **Answer**: **F**

### Q53 / Q54: Coded Blood Relations
- **Source Pages**: Pages 26, 54
- **Question Statement**:
  > If:
  > - $X \% Y$ means $X$ is the daughter of $Y$
  > - $X @ Y$ means $X$ is the son of $Y$

### Q68: In-Place Array Reversal Function

- **Question Statement**:
  > Consider the following function:
  > ```c
  > void Test(int arr[], int start, int end) {
  >     while (start < end) {
  >         int temp = arr[start];
  >         arr[start] = arr[end];
  >         arr[end] = temp;
  >         start++;
  >         end--;
  >     }
  > }
  > ```
  > What does the above code do?
- **Given Options**:
  - A) Swap all alternate elements of the array.
  - B) Traverse each alternate elements of the array.
  - C) Traverse each and every elements of the array.
  - D) Reverse the array.
- **Detailed Solution**:
  1. The function takes an array `arr`, with `start` initialized to the first index and `end` initialized to the last index.
  2. In each iteration of the loop, `arr[start]` and `arr[end]` are swapped using a temporary variable `temp`.
  3. `start` advances forward (`start++`) while `end` steps backward (`end--`).
  4. The process terminates when `start >= end`.
  5. This is the canonical two-pointer algorithm to **reverse an array in-place**.
- **Correct Answer**: **Reverse the array (Option D)**

### Q70: Max Heap Construction & Insertion of 50
- **Source Pages**: Page 62
- **Question Statement**:
  > Construct a max heap from the values: $35, 8, 88, 85, 45$.
  > Which one of the statements given in options is true when '$50$' is inserted into it?
- **Given Options**:
  - A) Height of heap is 3 (if root is at height 0)
  - B) 38, 5 and 45 will be the leaf nodes.
  - C) Sibling of 8 is 50.
  - D) 88, 85 and 50 are internal nodes.
- **Detailed Solution**:
  1. Initial sequential insertion of $35, 8, 88, 85, 45$:
     - Insert $35$: `[35]`
     - Insert $8$: `[35, 8]`
     - Insert $88$: `[35, 8, 88]` $\to$ swap with parent $35 \to$ `[88, 8, 35]`
     - Insert $85$: `[88, 8, 35, 85]` $\to$ swap with parent $8 \to$ `[88, 85, 35, 8]`
     - Insert $45$: `[88, 85, 35, 8, 45]` (child of $85$, $45 < 85$, valid)
  2. Now insert $50$:
     - $50$ is placed as the left child of $35$ (at index 5).
     - Since $50 > 35$, it violates the max-heap property and bubbles up, swapping with $35$.
     - Heap array becomes: `[88, 85, 50, 8, 45, 35]`.
  3. Tree structure:
     - Root: **88** (index 0)
     - Level 1: Left child = **85** (index 1), Right child = **50** (index 2)
     - Level 2: Children of 85 = **8, 45**; Child of 50 = **35**
  4. Node classification:
     - **Internal nodes** (nodes with at least one child): **88, 85, 50**
     - **Leaf nodes** (nodes without children): **8, 45, 35**
- **Correct Answer**: **88, 85 and 50 are internal nodes (Option D)**
